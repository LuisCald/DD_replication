\section*{0. Notation shared by both columns}
Three household measures $X^{(d)}$, $d\in\{C,Y,W\}$ (consumption, income, net worth), $D=3$. Quarters $t=1,\dots,T$ ($T=96$ in HANK). Surveys $j$; survey $j$ observes a subset of measures at a subset of dates.
Orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomials on $[0,1]$: $Q_m(u)=\sqrt{2m+1}\,P_m(2u-1)$, $m=0,\dots,K-1$, $K=12$ (order 11), $\int_0^1 Q_mQ_n\,du=\delta_{mn}$.

At each date the joint distribution is stored as a coefficient vector $y_t\in\R^N$:
\[
y_t=\big(\underbrace{\kappa_{\mathbf m,t}}_{\text{copula: }1694}\;,\;\underbrace{a^{(d)}_{m,t}}_{\text{marginals: }36}\big),\qquad N=1730,
\]
where $1694=K^D-K-(D-1)(K-1)$ after the uniform-margin constraints and $36=DK$.
Marginal coefficients (quantile function $\xi^{(d)}_t$, per-household mean $\bar X^{(d)}_t$ from the aggregates):
\[
a^{(d)}_{m,t}=\int_0^1 Q_m(u)\,\asinh\!\Big(\tfrac{\xi^{(d)}_t(u)}{\bar X^{(d)}_t}\Big)du
\;\approx\;\tfrac{1}{W}\textstyle\sum_i w_i\,Q_m(s_i)\,\asinh\!\big(X^{(d)}_i/\bar X^{(d)}_t\big),
\]
with $s_i$ the weighted cumulative rank of household $i$ in measure $d$ and $W=\sum_i w_i$.
Copula coefficients: $\kappa_{\mathbf m,t}=\E_t\big[\prod_d Q_{m_d}(U^{(d)})\big]$, $U^{(d)}=F^{(d)}_t(X^{(d)})$ the population rank; $\kappa_{\mathbf 0}=1$, pure-marginal terms $=0$.

State-space model with $r$ distributional factors $f_t$ and $q$ aggregate factors $a_t$:
\[
y_t=\Lambda\,\bar f_t+e_t,\quad e_t\sim N(0,\Sigma_t),\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}f_t\\a_t\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{t-1}\\a_{t-1}\end{pmatrix}+\varepsilon_t,\quad
\varepsilon_t\sim N(0,\Omega),
\]
with $\Omega=\diag(\omega)\,R\,\diag(\omega)$,
where $\bar f_t$ is the survey-specific lag weighting of $f_t$ (contemporaneous, or a tent over four quarters for annual surveys; the state carries $f_t,\dots,f_{t-3}$), $\Lambda\in\R^{N\times r}$ are the loadings (fixed, not sampled), $D$ is diagonal, $R$ an LKJ correlation matrix. $\Sigma_t$ has one free variance per (survey, object) broadcast over that object's coefficients. The likelihood is evaluated on the whitened system $\hat\Sigma^{-1/2}y_t,\ \hat\Sigma^{-1/2}\Lambda$ (see block 4).

\plain{The joint distribution of consumption, income and wealth at each quarter is written as 1{,}730 numbers. A handful of latent factors move those numbers over time; macro aggregates enter as additional factors. Everything below is about how those 1{,}730 numbers are built from surveys, how the factors are tied to them, and what is reported at the end.}

\section*{1. Copula ranks (data layer)}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{unweighted sample rank}
\old{
\[
\hat U^{(d)}_i=\frac{R^{(d)}_i}{n+1},\qquad R^{(d)}_i=\text{rank of }X^{(d)}_i\text{ among the }n\text{ sampled households}.
\]
\[
\hat\kappa_{\mathbf m}=\frac1W\sum_i w_i\prod_d Q_{m_d}\!\big(\hat U^{(d)}_i\big)
\]
The outer average is weighted; the ranks are not. $\hat U_i\to G^{(d)}(X_i)$, the CDF of the \emph{sample}, so the estimator converges to
\[
\E_F\Big[\textstyle\prod_d Q_{m_d}\big(\phi_d(U^{(d)})\big)\Big],\qquad \phi_d=G^{(d)}\!\circ (F^{(d)})^{-1},
\]
and $\phi_d\neq\mathrm{id}$ whenever the weights are unequal.
}
\plain{Each household's position in the queue was counted by rows. That is right only if every sampled household stands for the same number of real households. The SCF deliberately over-samples the rich, so the richest third of the sample is the richest one percent of the country and was placed a third of the way down the queue.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{weighted (population) rank}
\new{
\[
\hat U^{(d)}_{(i)}=\frac{n}{n+1}\cdot\frac{\sum_{j\le i}w_{(j)}}{W}\qquad\text{(data sorted ascending in }X^{(d)}).
\]
The Horvitz--Thompson empirical CDF, scaled so that it equals $R_i/(n+1)$ exactly when all weights are equal. Copula estimator otherwise unchanged. Same rule for the within-holder rank in the atom (stocks) branch.
}
\plain{Positions are now measured by accumulated weight, so the sampled rich occupy only the sliver of the queue they represent. Surveys with uniform weights are unchanged to the last digit. On the HANK SCF replica the mean wealth of the bottom half by income moved from $0.68$ to $0.31$ times average wealth against a truth of $0.31$; the top group from $2.37$ to $4.60$ against $4.59$. The real SCF has the same list-sample design, so this changes the paper's SCF copula.}

\medskip\textbf{Parameters:} none. Data construction only.
\end{paracol}

\section*{2. Trend}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{HP filter outside the model}
\old{
For every coefficient $i$ and survey $j$: linearly interpolate $y_{ijt}$ across the survey's observation dates, apply HP($\lambda=1600$) to obtain $\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}_{ijt}$, estimate the model on $c_{ijt}=y_{ijt}-\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}_{ijt}$, and add $\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}$ back at reconstruction:
\[
\hat y_{ijt}=\Lambda_i\,\hat{\bar f}_t+\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}_{ijt}.
\]
$\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}$ exists only over the survey's observed span; it carries no uncertainty and is invisible to the information decomposition.
}
\plain{The slow movement of every series was removed with a smoother before estimation and glued back afterwards. In recession windows the glued-back trend carried 58 to 100 percent of the headline features, with no error bands, and at the sample end the smoother's endpoint bias produced a Covid climb that the raw 2019 and 2021 waves contradict. Before 1999 the trivariate copula has no trend at all, so the 1962 extension was cycle-only.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{random-walk trend in factor space}
\new{
\[
\tau_t=\tau_{t-1}+\eta_t,\qquad \eta_t\sim N\big(0,\diag(\omega_\tau)\big),\quad \tau_t\in\R^{r},
\]
\[
y_t=\Lambda(\bar f_t+\bar\tau_t)+e_t .
\]
Same $\Lambda$ for trend and cycle: no new loadings. Transition augmented by $\tau$ with identity dynamics; the estimation's stationarity check applies to $A$ only. Global--local shrinkage on the trend innovations,
\[
\omega_{\tau,j}\sim N^+\!\big(0,(\lambda\psi_j)^2\big),\quad \psi_j\sim C^+(0,1),\quad \lambda\sim C^+(0,0.1).
\]
Data layer: no HP. Each coefficient row keeps a deterministic \emph{linear alignment drift} $\beta_{0,ij}+\beta_{1,ij}t$ fitted on its observed dates (absorbs survey-specific measurement drift); it is written back only from the row's first observation onward.
}
\plain{The trend is now a latent state estimated jointly with everything else. It lives in the same $r$-dimensional factor space and is read through the same loadings, so a coefficient's long-run drift is proportional to its cyclical sensitivity (a restriction, stated as such). It is identified at every date by whichever surveys are observed then, so the trivariate object acquires a trend back to 1962, filtered from data rather than extrapolated. It carries posterior uncertainty and enters the information decomposition. Marginal validation of $\tau$ against WID/DFA transfers to the joint because $\tau$ is shared.}

\medskip\textbf{New parameters:} $2r+1$ \ ($r$ innovation sds $\omega_\tau$, $r$ local scales $\psi$, one global scale $\lambda$). Plus $r$ new states.
\end{paracol}

\section*{3. Persistence prior and stationarity}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{one shared persistence target}
\old{
Minnesota prior on $\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}$ with own-lag prior mean $\rho_F$ on every distributional factor and $\rho_Y$ on aggregates; $\rho_F$ is a hyperparameter with its own prior, estimated. Factor innovation sds centred at $1-\rho_F^2$.
Hyperparameter block $H=(\kappa_1,\rho_F,\rho_Y,\sigma_\phi,\sigma_{\phi Y},\nu)$, six entries. No stationarity constraint.
}
\plain{All factors were told to be about equally persistent, and the common target was pulled to about $0.25$ by the income factors, which are noisy and fast. Wealth then inherited a fast, loud cycle it does not have. Nothing stopped the sampler from parking at an explosive mode: one run sat at a spectral radius of $1.38$, a disguised trend.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{per-factor targets, joint stationarity cap}
\new{
\[
\rho_{F,j}=0.99\tanh(\tilde\rho_j/2),\qquad \tilde\rho_j\sim N(3,1.5),\quad j=1,\dots,r,
\]
(centre $\approx0.90$); own-lag mean of $A$: $\diag(\rho_{F,1},\dots,\rho_{F,r})$; innovation sd centres $1-\rho_{F,j}^2$; $\sigma_\phi$ stays global.
\[
H=(\kappa_1,\rho_{F,1},\dots,\rho_{F,r},\rho_Y,\sigma_\phi,\sigma_{\phi Y},\nu)\in\R^{r+5}.
\]
Draws with $\max|\operatorname{eig}(\Phi)|>0.995$ on the \emph{joint} $\Phi$ are rejected; prior draws that violate it are shrunk onto the cap rather than discarded.
}
\plain{Each factor gets its own persistence, so income factors can be fast and wealth factors slow. On HANK the targets split to $0.14$ to $0.98$. The cap must be on the whole transition, not the factor block alone: with fewer aggregate factors the explosion hid in the aggregate equations.}

\medskip\textbf{New parameters:} $r-1$ \ (the $H$ block grows from 6 to $r+5$).
\end{paracol}

\section*{4. Whitening of the measurement equation}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{$\hat\Sigma^{-1/2}$ from the raw bootstrap covariance}
\old{
Bootstrap each survey-period ($B=999$ draws), detrend and standardise each draw, demean across draws, pool over dates:
\[
\hat\Sigma=\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}\big(y^{(b)}_{t}\big)\in\R^{N\times N},\qquad
\tilde y_t=\hat\Sigma^{-1/2}y_t,\ \ \tilde\Lambda=\hat\Sigma^{-1/2}\Lambda .
\]
The model's $\Sigma_t$ then needs only one variance per object. Unchanged as a device.
}
\plain{Coefficients are divided by their own sampling standard deviation and de-correlated, so one free variance per survey and object can stand in for 1{,}730 different measurement precisions. This is what lets the filter down-weight noisy high-order coefficients.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{ridge on $\hat\Sigma$ before inversion}
\new{
\[
\hat\Sigma_\lambda=\hat\Sigma+\lambda\,\overline{\diag(\hat\Sigma)}\,I,\qquad \lambda=0.05,\qquad
\tilde y_t=\hat\Sigma_\lambda^{-1/2}y_t .
\]
Caps the largest whitening weight at roughly $1/\lambda$ times the typical one. The prep prints $\cond(\hat\Sigma)$ per survey; on the HANK PSID it was $9\times10^{19}$, on the CEX $4\times10^{10}$.
\emph{Conditional on the empirical check:} apply if $\cond(\hat\Sigma)\gtrsim10^{12}$ on the real surveys. The diagonal alternative, whitening by $\diag(\hat\Sigma)^{-1/2}$ alone, is never singular and is a one-line change.
}
\plain{The bootstrap covariance was numerically singular. Coefficients that barely move across PSID waves have almost no sampling variance, so their whitening weight was effectively infinite; with loadings not built from PSID alone, they imposed thousands of near-exact zero constraints at every PSID wave and pinned the whole state to zero there. That was the source of the wave-date blips, the collapsed PSID-date correlations, and the $34\times$ measurement multiplier on PSID's copula. Under the ridge the multiplier fell to about $1$, all factors stayed alive, and the marginals were the best of the campaign.}

\medskip\textbf{Parameters:} none ($\lambda$ is a fixed tuning constant; the whitening matrix is data, not a parameter).
\end{paracol}

%======================================================================
\section*{5. Loadings $\Lambda$}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{PCA on complete-case columns}
\old{
Pool $X\in\R^{N\times M}$ of all survey-periods; keep columns with no missing entry, $X_{cc}$ ($M_{cc}\approx12$: the PSID waves, the only survey observing all three measures). PCA on $X_{cc}$; $r$ chosen by a variance-share rule (published: 99\%, giving $r=8$); scores $F$ have unit variance by construction; $\Lambda=$ top-$r$ eigenvectors.
}
\plain{The loadings that tie all 1{,}730 coefficients to the factors were determined by twelve columns, all from the PSID. Every other survey's information entered only through the filter, never through the loadings. The published variance rule saturates that pool.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{EM completion, unit scores, fixed validated $r$}
\new{
Keep every column observing $\ge5\%$ of rows ($M\approx236$). Iterate to convergence
\[
X^{(s+1)}_{\text{miss}}=\big[\operatorname{SVD}_r(X^{(s)})\big]_{\text{miss}},\qquad X_{\text{obs}}\text{ never changed},
\]
then PCA on the completed $X$. Rescale so the scores have unit variance on the complete-case columns,
\[
\Lambda\leftarrow\Lambda\diag(s),\qquad F\leftarrow\diag(s)^{-1}F,\qquad s_j=\operatorname{sd}\big(F_{j,\,cc}\big).
\]
Selection criteria are still reported on $X_{cc}$. $r$ is \emph{fixed} at the validated count (see block 8).
Optional refinement (Kalman-EM): given a mode, E-step $=$ smoother, M-step $=$ pooled regression of the raw rows observed by more than one survey on the smoothed factors, MAP-shrunk to $\Lambda_{\text{init}}$, damped; then re-estimate with the refined $\Lambda$.
}
\plain{All surveys' periods now shape the loadings, not just the PSID's twelve. The completed entries are the model's own low-rank prediction, so the rank cannot be chosen from the completed matrix; column hold-out cross-validation was built for that. The rescaling matters: without it the scores came out with standard deviation $34$ instead of $1$ and the Minnesota prior on the aggregate--factor blocks was off by a factor of about a thousand in both directions, silently, in every EM run before 17 September. Kalman-EM is the same idea using the estimated dynamics; it transfers because the real PSID is also the only complete-case survey.}

\medskip\textbf{Parameters:} none ($\Lambda$ remains fixed during sampling in both columns).
\end{paracol}

\section*{7. Estimation and reconstruction}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{}
\old{
Black-box optimisation (differential evolution) $\to$ DIME ensemble sampler, 400 iterations $\to$ mode $=$ best draw; marginal likelihood from the chain. Reconstruction from the smoothed factors,
\[
\hat y_t=\Lambda\,\hat{\bar f}_{t|T}+\tau^{\mathrm{HP}}_t ,
\]
then marginals by pointwise $\sinh$-inversion of the fitted quantile functions, copula density, integrals for quantile groups, shares, and conditional cells.
}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{}
\new{
Same sampler and mode. Reconstruction: $\hat y_t=\Lambda(\hat{\bar f}_{t|T}+\hat{\bar\tau}_{t|T})+\big(\beta_{0,ij}+\beta_{1,ij}t\big)$, the alignment drift only from a row's first observation (the constant-alignment output covers the full sample). Posterior factor draws now include $\tau$. The mode report prints the number of \emph{live} factors, innovation sd above $0.15\times$ the median.
}
\plain{The sampler is untouched. What changes is that the trend is part of what gets smoothed and what carries uncertainty. The black-box stage cannot be used to pick the factor count: at its candidate the innovation variances sit on their prior centre.}
\end{paracol}

\section*{8. Number of distributional factors}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{}
\old{Variance-share rule on the complete-case pool ($r=8$); alternatives by marginal data density.}
\plain{Fit criteria.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{}
\new{$r$ fixed, chosen by truth recovery in the validation exercise, with the live-factor count reported at the mode. Fit-based criteria are reported but do not select.}
\plain{On HANK the marginal likelihood rises monotonically in $r$ (four $\to$ eight: $+6{,}000$ log points) while recovery of the truth falls (bottom-half wealth by income $77\to57$, wealth marginals $-15$ to $-25$). The likelihood only sees observed dates; the model's value is the interpolation between them. More factors fit the observed points and pay for it in between. Four and five are equivalent on the cells the basis can carry; four has the cleaner mode and the better marginal likelihood. Structurally $r$ is not bounded by $q$; the only ceiling was the twelve complete columns, which EM lifts.}
\end{paracol}

\section*{9. Reported conditional cells}
\begin{paracol}{2}
\colhead{Old}{groups 50 / 40 / 10}
\old{
Marginal groups and cross-conditional groups both at $G\in\{[0,.5],[.5,.9],[.9,1]\}$:
\[
\E[X^{(o)}\mid U^{(c)}\!\in G]=\frac{\sum_{j,k}\kappa_{jk}\,A_j\,B_k(G)}{\sum_k\kappa_{0k}\,B_k(G)},
\]
\[
A_j=\int_0^1 Q_j(u)\,\xi^{(o)}(u)\,du,\qquad B_k(G)=\int_G Q_k(v)\,dv .
\]
}
\plain{Mean wealth of the top ten percent by income never correlated with the truth in any run, at any factor count.}
\switchcolumn
\colhead{New}{marginals 50 / 40 / 10, cross-conditionals 40 / 40 / 20}
\new{
Same operator, $G\in\{[0,.4],[.4,.8],[.8,1]\}$ for every $\E[X^{(o)}\mid U^{(c)}\!\in G]$ with $o\neq c$; the marginal share groups keep 50/40/10. The operator's truncation error is
\[
\textstyle\sum_{k\ge K}h_k\,B_k(G),\qquad h_k=\sum_j\kappa_{jk}A_j ,
\]
and for wealth as the outcome the ratio $|h_k/h_1|$ is $0.55$ at $k=11$ and $0.21$ at $k=30$, against $0.09$ and $0.015$ with income as the outcome: a steep function integrated over a window at the resolution limit $\approx1/K$.
Rule of thumb: for steep profiles keep each group wider than $\approx2/K$ of the rank axis. If a narrower group is wanted, the fix is a higher $K$ on the \emph{conditioning} axis only.
}
\plain{Mean wealth against income rank rises about fifteen-fold over the last tenth of the income distribution, and a degree-eleven polynomial cannot turn that corner inside a window one tenth wide. Widening the top group to a fifth makes the same estimated model recover the cells: four factors, wealth by income $82/66/74$, $80/66/74$, $82/63/75$ across the PSID, SCF and SIPP replicas, from $75/-2/11$ before. The basis places about a tenth of the middle group's wealth into the top fifth (levels $0.88$ and $1.05$ of truth, constant over time and identical across datasets and factor counts); compare levels to the truth-in-basis columns or state the bias.}

\medskip\textbf{Parameters:} none (reporting side).
\end{paracol}

\section*{10. Parameter counts}

\section*{11. Order of operations, new pipeline}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \new{Microdata $\to$ coefficients with weighted ranks} (block 1); marginals through $\asinh$ as before.
\item \new{Remove per-row linear alignment drift on observed dates} (no HP; block 2); demean; one $\sigma$ per object.
\item Bootstrap $\hat\Sigma$; \new{print $\cond(\hat\Sigma)$; ridge if needed} (block 4); whiten.
\item \new{EM completion $\to$ PCA at the fixed $r$ $\to$ unit-score rescale} (block 5); aggregates PCA, $q=11$.
\item Priors: Minnesota with \new{per-factor own-lag means}, LKJ, \new{trend hierarchy, joint stationarity cap} (blocks 2--3).
\item Black-box $\to$ DIME $\to$ mode; \new{live-factor report}; marginal likelihood (block 7).
\item Reconstruction with \new{$f+\tau$} and drift write-back; posterior draws \new{including $\tau$}.
\item Report: marginals at 50/40/10; \new{cross-conditionals at 40/40/20 via the continuous operator}; compare against WID/DFA (block 9).
\item Optional: \new{one Kalman-EM pass on the mode, then steps 4--8 again.}
\end{enumerate}
